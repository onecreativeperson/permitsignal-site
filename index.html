How it works

Public records, read every morning so you don't have to.

The information is already public. The problem is that nobody has time to read enforcement dockets every day. We do, and we send you only what matters in your counties.

STEP 1 · READ

We read the record

Formal and informal enforcement actions, notices of violation, penalty orders and quarterly non-compliance data from EPA ECHO and the state agencies (TCEQ, LDEQ, NCDEQ) for the counties you cover.

STEP 2 · SORT

We sort out what matters

Each new action is classified by what the facility will likely need – treatment upgrade, collection system, monitoring and reporting, permit work or stormwater – and rated for urgency using penalties, non-compliance status and 18 months of history.

STEP 3 · SEND

You get a one-page brief

Facility, action, penalty, the pattern behind it, the permit record and a plain-English line on what to offer. Only your counties. Nothing on quiet days.

Sources and fair use

Only the public record. Nothing scraped from private systems.

Permit Signal is built on the EPA's Enforcement and Compliance History Online (ECHO) datasets for the NPDES program – the same records anyone can download – and on state agency enforcement data as reported to EPA. We read them daily, structure them and add a plain-English reading of what the facility will likely need.

A brief is a pointer to a public record, not a judgment of the facility. Every brief links to the underlying ECHO facility report so you can check the record yourself. Permit Signal is not legal advice and is not affiliated with any agency.

Questions

Straight answers.

Is it legal to use this for business development?

Yes. Enforcement actions, permits and non-compliance reports are public records published by EPA and the state agencies precisely so that the public can see them. Permit Signal reads and organizes them; it does not access anything private.

How is this different from checking ECHO myself?

You can – it's all there. But ECHO is built for regulators and researchers, not for a contractor with ten minutes in the morning. We check every county you cover daily, filter out the noise, add 18 months of context and tell you what the facility will likely need, in one page.

What does "exclusive" mean?

For each county we take one subscriber per trade – one contract operator, one engineering firm, one compliance consultant, one stormwater firm. Your direct competitors in that county don't get the brief.

How current is the data?

EPA refreshes the national dataset about once a week; state actions usually appear within days to a few weeks of being issued. We read the data every morning, so you see a new action the day it is published – typically well before it turns into an RFP.

Do you send facility contact details?

We include what the public permit record lists – permit number, permit holder and the agency contact – plus a link to the ECHO facility report. We don't buy or scrape personal contact data.

How do I start and how do I cancel?

Email your county and what your firm does. You get the current week's signals for free, then two weeks of daily briefs at no charge. If it's useful, billing is monthly by card; cancel with one email.
